About

This is me, succinctly.

"I won't sue."

These were the words of my mother after suffering from medical malpractice, leaving her health irreversibly compromised. Stunned, I immediately asked "why?" -- that why has since fueled a lifelong desire to better understand both the pathophysiology of my mother's condition and the legal and tort systems that surrounded her case.

Outside of my school and research, I am a part of the Society of Asian Scientists and Engineers (SASE) at UF, where I served as the Member Involvement Chair from 2025-2026. I am also a part of the UF Carillon studio as a carillonneur, where I perform on the 61-bell carillon in Century Tower.

Research

Current Research Project

My current project focuses around hypertension, a leading modifiable risk factor for cardiovascular disease, among cancer survivors.

Hypertension in Cancer Survivors: Missed Opportunities for Treatment and Control in the United States

Conducted under Dr. Yenan Zhu Dept. of Health Services Research, Management & Policy College of Public Health & Health Professions, UF Affiliated since September 2025

Background

Hypertension is a common comorbidity among cancer survivors and a leading modifiable risk factor for cardiovascular disease. This study examined differences in untreated and uncontrolled hypertension among adults with and without cancer, and across major cancer types.

Methods

Data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) 2011–2020 were analyzed for adults, male and female who did not have a baby in the last 12 months, aged >= 20 years. Cancer history and hypertension awareness were identified by self-report. Uncontrolled hypertension was defined if systolic blood pressure stated >=130 mm Hg or diastolic blood pressure >=80 mm Hg, or if they were currently taking medication to lower blood pressure. Survey weights were applied to generate nationally representative estimates.

Results

Among 24,518 participants, 2,420 reported cancer (weighted N = 21,817,553) and 22,098 reported no cancer (weighted N = 176,511,556). Hypertension prevalence was higher among cancer survivors (62.06%) compared with non-cancer participants (37.94%). Hypertension awareness was also higher among those diagnosed with cancer (79.17%) compared to those without a history of cancer (70.27%). By cancer type, uncontrolled hypertension was highest among breast (64.09%) and colorectal (58.73%) and untreated hypertension was most common among breast cancer survivors (7.16%).

Conclusions

Cancer survivors, especially those with breast and colorectal cancer, face missed opportunities for hypertension treatment and control. Integrating systematic blood pressure screening, treatment optimization, and survivorship-focused cardiovascular care pathways may reduce preventable cardiovascular morbidity and improve long-term survival among cancer survivors.

My responsibilities

In this project, I collected and analyzed data from the NHANES (National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey) database using R, supporting the study's statistical analysis of hypertension treatment and control patterns across cancer survivor populations.
